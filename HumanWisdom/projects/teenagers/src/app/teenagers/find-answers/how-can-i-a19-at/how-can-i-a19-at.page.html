Cope with rejection

When you are a teenager it is normal to start having romantic feelings towards others. The society you live in, your parents, and your conditioning will largely dictate the norms of your behavior - whether you can go on dates, or not.

If you do start dating others you will have to deal with many emotions - excitement when you first meet someone, anxiety about what they really think about you and about whether they feel the same way as you do, uncertainty about whether to declare how you feel or play hard to get, insecurity about losing people, and so on. It can be a confusing time.

In this podcast we are going to explore how to deal with the pain of rejection - if your feelings are not reciprocated, or someone says they don’t want to be with you any more. It can be even worse if the other person says they love someone else. There are so many ways breakups can happen, but the pain they can cause is pretty much the same for everyone - so if this has happened to you, you are not alone. How can we make sense of what has happened and cope with the many feelings that arise?

The first thing to do is practice a breathing exercise - there are many to choose from in the app. They activate your parasympatheic nervous system and help you feel calm now. You need to do them regularly. However powerful the feelings you are experiencing, it's important to remind yourself that all feelings eventually pass and this will as well, and that this is part of life. You will be loved and adored again. You probably already are by many people, your friends and family for example, but take that for granted. Try to stay with the feeling, breathing into it, and not allowing it to translate into anger or shame or regret or guilt. Don’t worry what others are thinking - it does not matter and does not help.

The second thing is to zoom out and see this in perspective. If this is your first break up it can feel particularly painful, but it may not be your last. Just as we do not want to be held prisoner in any relationship and would like the freedom to leave when we want, we cannot insist others stay with us and need to offer them that freedom as well. If we love them we need to set them free. And just as we do not like everyone we meet, we cannot expect everyone we meet to like us either.

When someone says they don’t love you and want to leave you, it is not a judgment about you, but just the different ways of thinking you both have. Compatibility and chemistry are important, and cannot be manufactured. You can’t make yourself feel something towards another person, it has to come naturally.

Each of us will find that person we can be comfortable with, who understands us and loves us for who we are. For that to happen we need to also work on ourselves - be authentic, comfortable in our own skin, communicate with care and learn how to love others. None of these skills are easy, but they can all be learned if we apply ourselves - and the app offers resources for them all.

All relationships are an opportunity for learning, and this is an opportunity to go deeper, understand what you can learn about yourself and grow as a person, be more resilient, let people go with grace without ill will, and keep walking.
