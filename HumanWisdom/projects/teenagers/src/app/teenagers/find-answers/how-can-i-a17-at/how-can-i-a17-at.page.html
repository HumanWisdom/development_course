Make Better Decisions

We make so many decisions every day that impact our lives - some are daily ones - like what to wear, or eat, or how much to sleep, while others can be life-changing - for example what career to choose, whether to do something dishonest, whom to vote for, who to choose as a life partner, whether to take recreational drugs and so on.

How can we make better decisions so they serve us, and avoid making ones which can harm us in the long term?

We think we are in control of our own decisions but there are many factors we may not be aware of which influence the decisions we make. Being aware of them can help us make better choices.

These include our past conditioning influences, our fears, our self-interest, our need for pleasure and approval, our need to feel important and our need for power. They are all part of being human and not right or wrong, but operate often behind the scenes shaping the decisions we make.

For example our hidden fears may stop us from choosing a career we really love because we are afraid of what the future may hold.

Or, if we have made a mistake, we may be tempted to lie about what has happened, and it's the lie that comes back to bite us for the long term.

Our various conditioning influences may make take drugs, smoke or drink too much. We are not aware of the unconscious impact these have on our lives. Just waking up to them can help us make better choices.

Our need for approval from the people we respect may push us to do things that don’t really serve us in the long term. We may not be aware of this happening in the background.

All of these are explored separately and more fully in the app, and in the module that follows.

When making any decision that is going to impact our life, it's worth pausing and asking yourself a few questions: Why am I doing this? Is there another point of view to consider? What would happen if I did nothing? Is this decision in line with my values?

The pause is important to allow our rational mind to intervene - otherwise our emotions, our fears, and other factors can make our decisions for us.
